# Reflection and Career Planning

### Turning Career Exploration into Personal Action

---

# Overview

Career exploration becomes more valuable when learners actively interpret what they discover and connect it to their own goals.

For this reason, the Career Intelligence Lab incorporates structured reflection activities throughout the learning experience.

Rather than simply completing assessments, participants document observations, evaluate evidence, and create practical plans that can guide future learning and career decisions.

---

# Guided Reflection

Interactive H5P activities encourage learners to record their thinking as they progress through the course.

Reflection prompts invite participants to consider questions such as:

- What surprised me about this occupation?
- Which careers appear most compatible with my interests?
- What evidence supports my conclusions?
- Which knowledge and skills should I develop?
- Which occupations deserve further investigation?
- What actions can I begin taking today?

The emphasis is on developing thoughtful, evidence-based decisions rather than providing predetermined answers.

---

# Career Planning

As learners progress, they gradually build a personalised career portfolio that may include:

- Career exploration notes
- Personal reflections
- Skills gap analysis
- Career comparison summaries
- Mission statement
- Personal action plan
- Career development goals

These outputs become practical resources that learners can revisit and update as their interests, experience, and opportunities evolve.

---

# Downloadable Learning Portfolio

The H5P documentation activities allow learners to export their work as a Microsoft Word document.

This enables participants to:

- Save a personal copy of their reflections
- Continue developing their career plans outside the platform
- Share their work with teachers, mentors, or career advisors
- Build a personal career development portfolio

Learners may also transfer their reflections into the Moodle blog or personal learning journal, creating an ongoing record of their professional development.

# Authentic Learning

The reflection activities illustrate my approach to instructional design.

Rather than measuring success through quizzes alone, participants demonstrate learning by analysing evidence, articulating their thinking, and producing meaningful outputs that support future decision-making.

The result is a learning experience that encourages ownership, self-awareness, and continuous professional growth.
