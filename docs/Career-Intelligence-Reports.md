# Career Intelligence Reports

### Turning Occupational Data into Meaningful Career Insights

---

# Overview

One of the distinguishing features of the Career Intelligence Lab is the use of visual Career Intelligence Reports.

Rather than relying on career descriptions alone, learners analyse interactive reports that combine self-assessment, occupational information, labour market intelligence, and visual analytics to support evidence-based career decisions.

Each report transforms complex occupational data into practical insights that learners can interpret, compare, and discuss throughout the learning experience.

---

# What Learners Explore

Each occupation includes a rich collection of information that supports structured exploration.

Examples include:

- Career Overview
- Typical Daily Activities
- Knowledge Areas
- Skills and Competencies
- Work Styles
- Technology Requirements
- Labour Market Demand
- Bright Outlook indicators
- Green Occupation indicators
- Salary Information (where available)
- Career Videos
- Interactive Visualisations

---

# Visual Analytics

The reports combine multiple visual representations to help learners compare occupations more effectively.

These include:

- RIASEC Compatibility Radar Charts
- Knowledge Area Profiles
- Work Style Profiles
- Occupational Characteristics
- Labour Market Indicators
- Technology Requirements

Visualisation helps learners identify patterns that are often difficult to recognise from text alone.

---

# From Data to Decisions

The reports encourage learners to move beyond simply reading about occupations.

Instead, they investigate questions such as:

- Which occupations best match my interests?
- Which careers require skills I already possess?
- Which knowledge areas should I continue developing?
- Which occupations are expected to grow?
- Which technologies are commonly used?
- What alternative careers show similar characteristics?

The emphasis is on interpreting evidence rather than selecting careers based on assumptions.

# Learning Through Comparison

Rather than presenting a single "best" career, participants compare multiple occupations using the same evidence framework.

This comparative approach helps learners recognise transferable strengths, identify skill gaps, and explore alternative career pathways that may better align with both their interests and labour market opportunities.

- Workforce Analytics
