## Overview

**Career Intelligence Lab** is a working learning product designed to help people make more informed career decisions by combining:

**self-awareness + occupational data + labour-market information + interactive learning + reflection**

It was designed for delivery through **workshops and digital learning**, with a framework that can be adapted and scaled for different learner groups and contexts.

The initiative is particularly relevant for **young people entering the workforce**, career decision-making, career preparedness, and workforce-readiness programs.

The central idea is simple:

> **Better career decisions require more than knowing what jobs exist. People also need a realistic understanding of themselves, the skills work requires, and how the labour market is changing.**

---

## 🎯 What Participants Develop

Career Intelligence Lab helps participants move from general career ideas toward evidence-informed career direction.

### 🔎 Make Informed Career Decisions

Explore occupations using evidence rather than assumptions, and build a more informed understanding of possible career paths.

### 🧩 Understand the Skills Behind Jobs

Identify the technical knowledge, transferable skills, and capabilities associated with different occupations.

### 🎯 Connect Interests and Abilities to Career Options

Use self-assessment, reflection, and occupational information to explore options that align with individual interests, abilities, and preferences.

### 📊 Develop Labour-Market Awareness

Explore where opportunities are emerging and what available labour-market information suggests about future work.

### 🧭 Build a Personal Career Direction

Bring together self-assessment, occupational exploration, reflection, and evidence to develop practical next steps.

---

## 🧠 The Learning Challenge

A recurring challenge in career development is the gap between:

**how people understand their own capabilities**

and

**what work actually requires and how those requirements are changing.**

Career Intelligence Lab is designed to help make that gap more visible.

This is especially relevant as organizations increasingly expect people to work effectively with **digital tools, changing workflows, and AI-enabled ways of working**.

The purpose is not to predict someone's career.

It is to help the learner develop a **more realistic, evidence-informed view of possibilities, requirements, and next steps**.

---

## 🛠️ How the Learning Experience Works

The Lab combines several elements:

```text
Self-Awareness
      ↓
Career Exploration
      ↓
Occupational Evidence
      ↓
Labour-Market Awareness
      ↓
Reflection
      ↓
Career Direction
      ↓
Practical Next Steps
```

The learner is not simply given information.

The learning experience is designed to help participants **explore, analyse, reflect, and make decisions**.

# 👥 Who It Can Serve

The Career Intelligence Lab can be adapted for different contexts, including:

* young people entering the workforce
* career preparedness programs
* workforce-development initiatives
* educational institutions
* career guidance and employability programs
* employer-supported workforce initiatives
* collaborative career and learning workshops

The underlying framework is designed to be adaptable rather than tied to one learner population.

---

# 🌱 Why I Built It

I developed Career Intelligence Lab in response to the need for people to make career decisions using more than intuition or generic career advice.

The project brings together **career exploration, occupational data, self-awareness, reflection, and learning design** into one experience.

My broader interest is in creating learning environments that help people:

**analyse evidence → understand themselves → explore possibilities → make informed decisions → take practical action**

---

# 🔄 A Product Designed to Evolve

Career Intelligence Lab is a working initiative rather than a fixed theoretical model.

The framework can be adapted as:

* labour-market conditions change
* occupations evolve
* skill requirements shift
* digital tools change
* AI changes how work is performed
* new learner populations and delivery contexts emerge

This makes the Lab suitable for continued development and adaptation.
